\begin{abstract}
% No citations permitted in the abstract.
We ask whether, for a fixed containerized segmentation system, a commit-frozen multi-gate policy
audit can prevent adoption of an inference modification whose aggregate development-subset benefit
does not transfer consistently to a same-corpus policy-selection holdout. GAT-26 is a five-fold
region-based residual-encoder nnU-Net trained only on subchallenge data from a recorded random
initialization. Our contribution is procedural rather than architectural: the inference policy that
ships is the object under test, and candidates, utility and gates were fixed in a pushed commit
before the specified comparison was computed. Eightfold mirroring improved all six ranked components
on the development folds, then weakened on the holdout. The aggregate utility gain fell from
$+0.00253$ to $+0.00073$ at surface tolerance $\tau=1$, and from $+0.00417$ to $+0.00247$ at
$\tau=0.5$; the bootstrap interval spanned zero at both tolerances, one holdout fold changed sign,
and the reference-lesion miss rate regressed by $+0.0070$ against a $0.0025$ operational margin, so
the simpler baseline was retained. We also document a discrepancy that is easy to ship unnoticed:
our internal cross-validation used eightfold mirroring while the released container uses none, so
the two are not comparable. This is a bounded single-system case study, not evidence that the
procedure is preferable to nested validation.

\section{Introduction}
The Brain Tumor Segmentation (BraTS) benchmark has standardized evaluation of automated glioma
segmentation from multiparametric MRI~\cite{menze2015brats,bakas2017advancing,baid2021brats}, and has
expanded into subchallenges covering meningioma~\cite{bratsmen2023}, brain
metastases~\cite{bratsmet2023}, pediatric tumors~\cite{bratsped2023} and an under-represented
Sub-Saharan Africa patient population~\cite{bratsssa2023}, scored by containerized
infrastructure~\cite{karargyris2023medperf}. BraTS-GoAT poses the cross-tumor question directly: one
model must segment enhancing tumor (ET), tumor core (TC) and whole tumor (WT) across heterogeneous
tumor types and acquisition sources, with no per-cohort tuning and no cohort label at inference time.

Strong baselines are well established, and the self-configuring nnU-Net family remains hard to beat
on BraTS-style tasks~\cite{isensee2021nnunet,isensee2024nnunetrevisited}, so we did not pursue a new
architecture. What interested us instead is that a challenge submission is not a model but a
\emph{container}, and that the inference policy inside it---checkpoint selection, test-time
augmentation, tiling overlap, thresholding, post-processing, ensembling---is routinely chosen on the
same data used to report cross-validated performance, with no holdout and no advance commitment.
Underspecification makes this fragile: pipelines equivalent on a development distribution can differ
elsewhere~\cite{damour2022underspecification}, the regime a generalization task probes.

\paragraph{Research question.} We ask one question: \emph{for a fixed containerized segmentation
system, can a commit-frozen, multi-gate policy audit prevent adoption of an inference modification
whose aggregate development-subset benefit does not transfer consistently to a same-corpus
policy-selection holdout?} We answer it as a bounded case study on one system, one corpus and one
split, and we do not claim it shows the procedure is generally superior to nested cross-validation or
to an external cohort.

\paragraph{Transferable conclusions.} Three claims survive our evidence. First, \emph{release-path
equivalence must be verified before inference policies are compared}: our internal cross-validation
numbers and our shipped container came from different paths, which silently offsets every internal
figure. Second, \emph{a small aggregate utility gain can coexist with fold heterogeneity and worse
lesion-miss behaviour}, so an aggregate mean is not sufficient evidence to adopt an inference change.
Third, \emph{a same-corpus policy holdout reduces direct post-result tuning but does not replace
nested validation or an external independent cohort}.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data and Task}
We use only data supplied through the 2026 BraTS-GoAT subchallenge, which draws on the BraTS data
lineage~\cite{menze2015brats,bakas2017advancing,baid2021brats}, its expert-annotated public
collections~\cite{bakas2017gbm,bakas2017lgg} and its cohort-specific
releases~\cite{bratsmen2023,bratsmet2023,bratsped2023,bratsssa2023}. The training release comprises
1{,}351 labeled cases, which our audit found complete, geometry-valid and integer-labeled. The
evaluated regions are the standard nested triple $\mathrm{ET}=[3]$, $\mathrm{TC}=[1,3]$,
$\mathrm{WT}=[1,2,3]$ over $\{0{=}\text{bg},1{=}\text{NCR},2{=}\text{ED},3{=}\text{ET}\}$. Validation
and test ground truth is withheld. Per-case cohort labels were not reliably derivable from the
released naming or metadata and we did not guess them, so cohort-stratified analysis lies outside this
work---a real limitation for a task about cohort transfer.

\subsection{Split, Preprocessing and Training}
We build a deterministic group-level five-fold split (seed $21072026$; fold sizes 271, 270, 270, 270
and 270). A duplicate and near-duplicate analysis found no confirmed same-subject pairs, so cases are
treated as singleton groups; we claim only that the audit found no confirmed duplicate group, not that
same-subject leakage is impossible. Every fold contains ET-absent and TC-absent cases, exercising the
empty-region paths the evaluator must handle. The split was frozen before training and never changed.

Preprocessing is the standard nnU-Net v2.8.1 pipeline over the four modalities (T1n, T1c, T2w, T2f)
in a fixed channel order, using nnU-Net's own fingerprint and plans. The backbone is the ResEnc-M
residual-encoder U-Net in \texttt{3d\_fullres} with region-based ET/TC/WT targets, patch size
$[128,160,112]$, batch size 2, SGD with Nesterov momentum, initial learning rate $10^{-2}$ with
polynomial decay, 0.33 foreground oversampling and deep supervision, trained 1000 epochs per fold with
the final checkpoint retained; the full plan is in the public repository. All trainable parameters
begin from a recorded random initialization; no pretrained or self-supervised weights are used.
Folds train independently, and all five are complete.

\subsection{Architecture Selection, and Its Metric}
\label{sec:arch}
Selection between the ResEnc-M and ResEnc-L presets followed a policy fixed before any fold result
was seen: the utility is the mean fractional rank over six equally weighted components, ties resolve
to the smaller model, and ResEnc-L is preferred only on a meaningful fold-0 benefit---an award-rank
gain $\ge 1/6$, a paired-bootstrap interval favouring L, and all declared noninferiority gates
passing. The screen used fold~0 only, and paired DSC with HD95 because it was fixed before the
ranking metric was clarified. The challenge ranks the Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) and the normalized surface distance
(NSD); HD95 is
diagnostic. Section~\ref{sec:arch2} rescores that comparison under DSC/NSD.

\subsection{Release Inference Path}
Final inference is a sequential five-checkpoint mean-probability ensemble: one fold model is resident
at a time and freed before the next, so peak memory is that of a single model. Region probabilities
are thresholded at a fixed $0.5$---no learned threshold or ensemble weight, no test-time
augmentation, no connected-component filtering, no presence gate---and reconstructed by a
hierarchy-safe projection enforcing $\mathrm{WT}\supseteq\mathrm{TC}\supseteq\mathrm{ET}$, restoring
the exact source geometry. Across two independent 24-case runs, 18 of $2.14\times10^{8}$ output voxels
differed: the runner is near-deterministic but not bit-exact, because deterministic-algorithm
enforcement runs in warn-only mode.

\subsection{Decision Procedure for Policy Audits}
\label{sec:procedure}
Candidate evaluation is \emph{staged} (Fig.~\ref{fig:workflow}). Each audit compares one frozen
candidate against the frozen release baseline under rules fixed in advance, using the organizers'
evaluation package with explicit surface tolerances $\tau\in\{0.5,1.0\}$; a candidate failing its
advancement criteria stops on the development subset and is not carried further.

\paragraph{Utility.} Let $c$ index the six ranked components (ET/TC/WT $\times$ DSC/NSD). Each
component mean is converted to a fractional rank $r_c(P)\in\{0,\tfrac12,1\}$ within the two-policy
comparison (higher is better, ties $\tfrac12$), and
\begin{equation}
U(P)=\frac{1}{6}\sum\nolimits_{c=1}^{6} r_c(P),\qquad
\Delta U = U(P_{\mathrm{cand}}) - U(P_{\mathrm{base}}),
\end{equation}
an unweighted mean: DSC and NSD enter with equal weight, no region is up-weighted, aggregation is at
case level then averaged, and HD95 never enters $U$.

\paragraph{Common support and fail-closed evaluation.} A region-case contributes only if both
policies yield a finite value, so a change in the number of scorable cases cannot by itself create a
gain; we report the common-support size beside every $\Delta U$. Empty reference regions follow the
evaluator's convention and non-finite entries are excluded rather than imputed. Common support is a
comparability device, not an error-tolerance device: a candidate whose expected evaluation is
\emph{missing}, \emph{errored} or \emph{membership-mismatched} is ineligible, and the audit fails
closed rather than silently comparing whatever subset survived. Three of the twenty-three holdout
checks enforce exactly this---exact membership, zero evaluator errors and independent recomputation
agreement---and all three passed.

\paragraph{Uncertainty and safety.} Uncertainty is a paired \emph{subject-level} bootstrap---subjects
are resampled, never region-cases---with seed $20260730$ and $10{,}000$ resamples, summarized by the
fraction of resamples with $\Delta U>0$ and a percentile interval; that fraction is the share of
resamples in which the candidate is ahead, not a posterior probability. Ties resolve to the baseline.
Safety is assessed on 26-connected lesion components in the spirit of instance-wise
evaluation~\cite{kofler2023panoptica}, and metric choice materially affects such
conclusions~\cite{reinke2024metricsreloaded}: a reference component counts as missed if no predicted
component of the same region overlaps it, and the miss rate is the fraction missed, a denominator
depending only on the reference. The frozen noninferiority margins were $0.0025$ on the point change
in miss rate, $0.0050$ on its one-sided 95\% upper bound and $0.0050$ per region, with a 5\% cap on
the relative increase in predicted false-positive components. These are \emph{operational} margins,
strict relative to the observed between-fold variation of the quantity they bound; they are not
externally validated clinical thresholds.

\subsection{Container Contract}
\label{sec:contract}
The submission is a \texttt{linux/amd64} zero-network container built for the official Task-3 budget
(NVIDIA A10G, 24\,GB VRAM, 12\,h), with all dependencies and all five fold checkpoints baked in at
build time. It reads a read-only \texttt{/input} and writes a flat \texttt{/output} holding one
\texttt{.nii.gz} per case folder. \textbf{Each output file name is the complete validated basename of
its input case folder}, with no truncation to a fixed-width identifier and no hardcoded cohort
prefix; an earlier revision that assumed a fixed-width suffix was corrected, and the release
preflight now tests multiple prefixes and variable-length basenames. Labels are restricted to
$\{0,1,2,3\}$, geometry is preserved exactly, and strict modality discovery rejects missing,
duplicate, unknown and unreadable modalities before any output is written. The official contract
supplies a fresh writable \texttt{/output}; we make no claim that the runner refuses to overwrite an
existing output file. Operational detail lives in the public repository. The source is public under
the Apache License 2.0 at the immutable tag
\url{https://github.com/Neethan-hub/gat26-brats-2026/tree/brats-goat-2026-camera-ready-21}. It
contains no images, labels, checkpoints or predictions, so it does not reproduce training without
separately obtained challenge data and the private trained weights.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Architecture Selection and Descriptive Out-of-fold Performance}
\label{sec:oof}
On the frozen fold-0 validation set (271 cases) ResEnc-M scored ET/TC/WT DSC of $0.859$, $0.914$ and
$0.934$ against ResEnc-L at $0.861$, $0.912$ and $0.932$, winning four of six components under the
DSC/HD95 screen; the paired bootstrap did not favour ResEnc-L (rank-difference interval
$[-0.667,+0.667]$) and ResEnc-L failed both declared tail gates, so the policy selected ResEnc-M.

We assembled a strictly out-of-fold set in which each case is predicted once, by the fold model that
did not train on it: the union covers 1{,}351 cases, each exactly once, with no missing case, no
duplicate and no fold overlap. Scored in one pass by the official evaluator with zero errors and all
six denominators equal to 1{,}351, it gives DSC $0.865$ (ET), $0.915$ (TC), $0.927$ (WT) and HD95
$13.39$, $6.75$ and $6.34$\,mm.

Two qualifications are essential. This is \emph{descriptive post-selection out-of-fold performance,
conditional on the chosen model family and split}---neither an unbiased generalization estimate nor
in-sample training accuracy: fold~0 chose the architecture family and then appears inside this
summary, and the corpus was reused across audits, so adaptive selection and multiplicity can both
create optimism. And it is not the released ensemble's score and cannot be, since every training case
trained four of the five constituent models; it also came from a different inference path.

\subsection{Rescoring the Architecture Comparison under the Ranking Metric}
\label{sec:arch2}
Because the architecture screen used DSC/HD95, we rescored the same fold-0 predictions under DSC/NSD
at $\tau=0.5$ as a diagnostic. The ordering \emph{changes at the point estimate}: ResEnc-L is better
on four of six official components (ET DSC $0.860$ vs $0.858$; ET NSD $0.668$ vs $0.657$; TC NSD
$0.624$ vs $0.621$; WT NSD $0.576$ vs $0.576$), while ResEnc-M remains better on TC and WT DSC. The
rank gain for L is $+0.333$, but the 95\% interval $[-1.000,+0.667]$ includes a tie, so the declared
robustness requirement was not met and no ResEnc-L retraining was performed. The diagnostic was
computed at $\tau=0.5$ only, uses each architecture's own end-of-training predictions rather than the
release path, and is a fold-0 comparison. We therefore do \emph{not} claim the architecture conclusion
is unchanged under the ranking metric: the point ordering reverses, the reversal is not robust, and
the deployed model was selected under a metric pair the challenge does not rank on. This
architecture--metric mismatch is a limitation, not a resolved question.

\subsection{Two Inference Paths}
\label{sec:twopaths}
Every internal figure above came from the official evaluator applied to nnU-Net's own end-of-training
validation predictions, whose recorded settings include mirroring axes $(0,1,2)$: eightfold mirroring
test-time augmentation. The released container disables mirroring entirely. We found this by trying
to reproduce an internal development baseline through the release code path and failing. On the same
811 development subjects, with the same evaluator, tolerances and denominators, the augmented lineage
reproduces the recorded internal values to a worst absolute error of $4.3\times10^{-7}$, whereas the
release path is lower by roughly $0.002$ on each DSC component and $0.002$--$0.007$ on each NSD
component. The gap is single-signed across all twelve component/tolerance combinations and reproduces
fold by fold---the signature of augmentation, not of nondeterminism. No internal number here therefore
estimates what the released container produces.

\subsection{Checkpoint and Weight-averaging Audits}
\label{sec:audits}
\textbf{Audit~A} tested the best-validation checkpoint, mirroring over the recorded axes, both
combined, and ET component cleanup at three declared volume thresholds, on development folds 0--2
($n=811$). No candidate improved robustly. \textbf{Audit~B} tested two declared per-fold weight
averages of the final and best-validation checkpoints, $S_1=0.75\theta_f+0.25\theta_b$ and
$S_2=0.50\theta_f+0.50\theta_b$~\cite{wortsman2022modelsoups}; it is the sharpest negative, with all
six ranked components moving the wrong way for both soups, monotonically in the amount mixed in. Both
audits stopped on the development subset, leaving folds 3--4 untouched.

\subsection{Mirroring: a Development Gain That Did Not Transfer}
\label{sec:auditc}
Audit~C tested a single candidate, M8, differing from the baseline C0 only in that mirroring is
enabled over axes exactly $(0,1,2)$, executed end-to-end by the release machinery on raw NIfTI
inputs. One scoping point is essential: here each subject is predicted by \emph{its one excluded-fold
checkpoint}, whereas the deployed submission ensembles \emph{all five}. The audit tests a closely
related inference-policy effect; it does not directly measure mirroring on the deployed five-model
ensemble.

On the development subset, M8 improved all six ranked components at both tolerances
(Table~\ref{tab:auditc}), and seventeen of eighteen frozen checks passed. The one failure was a strict
veto on any increase in lesion false-negative \emph{count}, which rose by 13 ($+0.3\%$); under that
frozen gate the audit retained the baseline. Inspecting the outcome exposed a limitation of the veto
rather than an error in it: the number of reference lesion components is \emph{policy-invariant}, but
an absolute count does not normalize by that fixed number of opportunities and carries no uncertainty
interval. We therefore fixed, in a pushed commit, a separate reference-lesion miss-rate
noninferiority analysis (Section~\ref{sec:procedure}). It was commit-frozen before the holdout
comparison but is not globally result-blind---it was designed after seeing the development outcome,
and we say so plainly---and it does not annul the earlier audit, which correctly applied its own
frozen rule.

\paragraph{Holdout outcome, at both tolerances.} The $\tau=1$ gain fell from $+0.00253$ to $+0.00073$,
below the required $+0.0010$, with the positive-resample fraction falling from $1.000$ to $0.734$ and
a 95\% interval $[-0.0025,+0.0026]$ straddling zero. At $\tau=0.5$ the gain fell from $+0.00417$ to
$+0.00247$, with a positive fraction of $0.961$ and an interval $[-0.0003,+0.0042]$ that also includes
zero. Descriptively, the holdout retained $28.7\%$ of the development gain at $\tau=1$ and $59.3\%$ at
$\tau=0.5$; we report both because the official tolerance was never exposed, and neither ratio should
be read as the effect size. The two holdout folds gave $+0.0023$ and $-0.0009$---a sign change---and
the reference-lesion miss rate regressed by $+0.0070$ against the $0.0025$ margin, with a one-sided
95\% upper bound of $+0.0124$ against the $0.0050$ margin and all three regions over their own limits.
Eight of 23 holdout checks failed, including the primary utility and safety gates. No denominator and
no non-finite pattern changed, so neither the residual gain nor the failure is a denominator artifact,
and the released no-augmentation policy was retained.

\paragraph{What failed, qualitatively.} We publish no patient-level examples, so we characterize
failure in aggregate. Mirroring consistently \emph{reduced} predicted false-positive lesion components
(development $1243\rightarrow1087$; holdout $864\rightarrow781$) while consistently \emph{increasing}
missed reference components ($4103\rightarrow4116$; $2726\rightarrow2764$), and zero-DSC region cases
rose from 24 to 26 on the holdout. Averaging over eight flips smooths the probability field,
suppressing small spurious components but also pushing small true ones below the fixed $0.5$
threshold---a trade invisible in the overlap means, which improved, and visible only at instance
level. The absolute miss rate is high ($0.501\rightarrow0.508$) because every reference component
counts equally regardless of size; the gates bound the \emph{change}, not the level.

\paragraph{Bounded experiment inventory.} A connected-component proposal was rejected when its
apparent gain proved to come from changed ET support. A 40-epoch fine-tuning screen of three
variants---a tumor-type-mixture schedule, a domain-generalization augmentation schedule and their
combination---failed its frozen gates; bounded at 40 epochs, it does not establish how such schedules
would behave trained to convergence. A dense-overlap variant was \emph{not} tested. We did not test
larger ensembles, cascades, cohort routing or alternative thresholds.

These values are substantially below our internal cross-validation figures, and we do not explain the
gap away. One component is identified: the inference-path difference of Section~\ref{sec:twopaths}

to any specific mechanism. Per-case cohort labels were not derivable, so we cannot test tumor-type
transfer, and we have no subgroup results or controlled comparison. Domain shift, case composition,
ET-specific biological heterogeneity, adaptive reuse of the training corpus and scorer differences are
all consistent with what we observe, and our evidence does not separate them. We therefore report the
gap as a measured fact and decline to attribute it---including to cross-tumor transfer, the
attribution our own framing would most invite.

\subsection{Runtime Evidence}
\label{sec:container}
Our full-cohort evidence is the run of the exact release inference policy whose predictions produced
Table~\ref{tab:official}: 451 of 451 official validation cases, exit code $0$, zero inference errors,
peak reserved VRAM $2.48$\,GiB, wall time 3\,h\,35\,m ($28.6$\,s per case) on a single NVIDIA A40.
Runtime on one GPU does not transfer to another, but memory is far from binding---$2.48$\,GiB against
a 24\,GB budget---because the ensemble runs sequentially. An earlier, superseded revision of the
container was additionally exercised on one NVIDIA A10G using synthetic volumes. That test does not
qualify the corrected image we finally submitted: its fixtures used fixed-width folder names and never
exercised the variable-length condition the output-name correction addresses. We hold \emph{no} direct
A10G measurement of the corrected image, no organizer execution log, and no hidden-test evidence. This
bounds what can be said about augmentation and compute: eightfold mirroring multiplies forward passes
per tile by eight, and we measured neither its full-ensemble runtime nor its memory profile on the
target platform, so we make no feasibility claim in either direction.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Our quantities are not interchangeable: out-of-fold cross-validation is descriptive and
post-selection, produced by the augmented internal path; audit results are policy-selection
comparisons, not performance estimates; the single official measurement came from the release path on
a withheld cohort and carries no rank; and hidden-test performance, which alone determines the
ranking, does not exist at the time of writing, so we report and estimate none.

Returning to our question: the procedure did what it was built to do. A candidate that improved all
six ranked components on the development subset, with every bootstrap resample positive, was stopped
before release by gates fixed in advance. But this is one demonstration on one system: we have not
shown that the gates generalize, that the margins suit another model or corpus, or that this procedure
outperforms nested cross-validation, which remains preferable where feasible and which we did not run.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The policy holdout is same-corpus: folds 3--4 carry prior development
history, so they bound direct post-result tuning of this comparison, not adaptive reuse of the corpus,
and multiplicity across our audits is uncontrolled. Folds 3--4 are a policy-selection holdout, not an external independent cohort, and we hold none. The
architecture was selected under DSC/HD95 while the challenge ranks DSC/NSD, and the rescoring in
Section~\ref{sec:arch2} reverses the point ordering without robustness. The mirroring audit is a
single-model out-of-fold comparison and does not determine how mirroring would affect the deployed
five-checkpoint ensemble: averaging five models could mitigate, preserve or amplify the lesion-miss
behaviour we observed, so we neither adopted mirroring nor claim it would have helped. One submitted
validation prediction is absent from the organizers' per-case output for undisclosed reasons. Per-case
cohort labels were not reliably derivable, so no cohort-stratified analysis was possible in a task
about cohort transfer. We hold no hidden-test evidence, no rank, and no direct A10G qualification of
the corrected submitted image, and we did not run the comparison that would show whether the procedure
outperforms nested validation. Group-level splitting cannot prove the absence of same-subject leakage,
and a small minority of cases dominates the boundary-error mean. GAT-26 trains only on GoAT-provided
data from a recorded random initialization, with no external, prior-BraTS, private or pretrained data
or weights; validation and test labels were never used, and we performed no pseudo-labeling,
transductive adaptation or leaderboard-driven tuning.

\section{Conclusion}
GAT-26 is a five-fold region-based residual-encoder nnU-Net ensemble evaluated under commit-frozen
selection rules. Two audits stopped on the development subset; the mirroring audit advanced to a
same-corpus policy-selection holdout, where its gain weakened at both tolerances, its interval spanned
zero, one fold changed sign and a lesion miss-rate gate failed. All three retained the simpler
baseline. Two observations travel further than the architecture: internal cross-validation numbers and
the shipped container came from different inference paths, and a development-subset gain that looked
unambiguous transferred only partially---$28.7\%$ at $\tau=1$, $59.3\%$ at $\tau=0.5$---while
lesion-miss behaviour worsened. Official validation was well below our internal figures, a gap we
report without attributing. This is a bounded case study; nested or external validation remains
preferable where feasible.
