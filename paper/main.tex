\begin{abstract}
% No citations permitted in the abstract.
We ask whether, for a fixed containerized segmentation system, a commit-frozen multi-gate policy
audit can prevent adoption of an inference modification whose aggregate development-subset benefit
does not transfer consistently to a same-corpus policy-selection holdout. GAT-26 is a five-fold region-based
residual-encoder nnU-Net trained only on subchallenge data from a recorded random initialization. Our
contribution is procedural: candidate inference modifications were executed end-to-end through the
release machinery on excluded-fold predictions, with candidates, utility and gates fixed in a pushed
commit before the specified comparison. The utility is the unweighted mean of six raw component
means, ET/TC/WT by DSC and NSD, on common subject support. Eightfold mirroring improved all six
DSC/NSD components on the development folds but failed a frozen lesion false-negative veto and
stopped. A separately commit-frozen follow-up opened the untouched holdout folds; the gain was
descriptively smaller: $+0.00073$ against $+0.00253$ at the official-ranking tolerance, $+0.00247$
against $+0.00417$ in a lower-tolerance sensitivity analysis. The holdout bootstrap intervals spanned
zero across tolerances, one fold changed sign, and the reference-lesion miss rate regressed by
$+0.0070$ against a $0.0025$ operational margin, so the baseline was retained. We document a
discrepancy easy to ship unnoticed: our cross-validation used eightfold mirroring while the released
container does not, so they are not comparable. This is a bounded case study, not evidence the
procedure is preferable to nested validation.

\section{Introduction}
The BraTS benchmark standardized evaluation of automated glioma segmentation from multiparametric
MRI~\cite{menze2015brats,bakas2017advancing,baid2021brats} and has expanded into subchallenges
covering meningioma~\cite{bratsmen2023}, brain metastases~\cite{bratsmet2023}, pediatric
tumors~\cite{bratsped2023} and a Sub-Saharan Africa population~\cite{bratsssa2023}, scored by
containerized infrastructure~\cite{karargyris2023medperf}. BraTS-GoAT poses the cross-tumor question directly: one
model must segment enhancing tumor (ET), tumor core (TC) and whole tumor (WT) across heterogeneous
tumor types and sources, with no per-cohort tuning and no cohort label at inference.

The self-configuring nnU-Net family remains hard to beat on BraTS-style
tasks~\cite{isensee2021nnunet,isensee2024nnunetrevisited}, so we did not pursue a new architecture.
What interested us is that a challenge submission is a \emph{container}, and that its inference
policy (checkpoint, test-time augmentation, tiling overlap, threshold, post-processing,
ensembling) is routinely chosen on the same data used to report cross-validated performance, with no
holdout and no advance commitment. Underspecification makes this fragile: pipelines equivalent on a
development distribution can differ elsewhere~\cite{damour2022underspecification}.

\paragraph{Research question.} We ask: \emph{for a fixed containerized segmentation system, can a
commit-frozen, multi-gate policy audit prevent adoption of an inference modification whose aggregate
development-subset benefit does not transfer consistently to a same-corpus policy-selection
holdout?} We answer it as a bounded case study on one system, corpus and split, and do not claim it
shows the procedure is generally superior to nested cross-validation or an external cohort.

\paragraph{Transferable conclusions.} Three claims survive our evidence. \emph{Release-path
equivalence must be verified before inference policies are compared}---ours was not, which silently
offsets every internal figure. \emph{A small aggregate gain can coexist with fold heterogeneity and
worse lesion-miss behaviour}, so an aggregate mean is not sufficient evidence to adopt a change. And
\emph{a same-corpus policy holdout reduces direct post-result tuning}, but it is
not an external independent cohort and does not replace nested validation.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data and Task}
We use only data supplied through the 2026 BraTS-GoAT subchallenge, which draws on the BraTS data
lineage~\cite{menze2015brats,bakas2017advancing,baid2021brats}, its expert-annotated public
collections~\cite{bakas2017gbm,bakas2017lgg} and its cohort-specific
releases~\cite{bratsmen2023,bratsmet2023,bratsped2023,bratsssa2023}. The training release comprises
1{,}351 labeled cases, which our audit found complete, geometry-valid and integer-labeled; evaluated
regions are the standard nested triple $\mathrm{ET}=[3]$, $\mathrm{TC}=[1,3]$, $\mathrm{WT}=[1,2,3]$.
Validation and test ground truth is withheld. Per-case cohort labels were not reliably derivable and
we did not guess them, so cohort-stratified analysis lies outside this work---a real limitation for a
task about cohort transfer.

\subsection{Split, Preprocessing and Training}
We build a deterministic group-level five-fold split (seed $21072026$; fold sizes 271, 270, 270, 270,
270), frozen before training and never changed. A duplicate analysis found no confirmed same-subject
pairs, so cases are singleton groups; we claim only that the audit found no confirmed duplicate
group, not that leakage is impossible. Every fold contains ET-absent and
TC-absent cases, exercising the empty-region paths the evaluator must handle.

Preprocessing is the standard nnU-Net v2.8.1 pipeline over the four modalities (T1n, T1c, T2w, T2f)
in fixed channel order. The backbone is the ResEnc-M residual-encoder U-Net in
\texttt{3d\_fullres} with region-based ET/TC/WT targets, patch size $[128,160,112]$, batch size 2,
SGD with Nesterov momentum, weight decay $3\times10^{-5}$, initial learning rate $10^{-2}$ with
polynomial decay, 0.33 foreground
oversampling and deep supervision, trained 1000 epochs per fold with the final checkpoint retained;
the full plan is in the public repository. All parameters begin from a recorded random
initialization; no pretrained or self-supervised weights are used. Folds train independently, and all
five are complete.

\subsection{Architecture Selection and Its Metric}
\label{sec:arch}
Selection between the ResEnc-M and ResEnc-L presets followed a policy fixed before any fold result
was seen, on fold~0 only, using DSC and HD95. Its statistic differs from the audit utility. For each
of the six screened components the presets are ranked against each other, better value first (higher
DSC, lower HD95), exact ties taking the average of the tied positions; $R$ is the mean of those six
fractional ranks, so \emph{lower $R$ is better}. We report $\Delta R=R(\mathrm{L})-R(\mathrm{M})$,
negative favouring ResEnc-L, with a paired subject-level bootstrap (seed $21072026$, $10{,}000$
resamples) summarized by a 95\% percentile interval. ResEnc-L could advance from fold~0 to fold-1
confirmation only if the executable rank-gain threshold on $R(\mathrm{M})-R(\mathrm{L})$, nominally
$1/6$, was met, the interval for $\Delta R$ lay entirely below zero, and every frozen auxiliary gate
was supplied and passed; expansion required the same on fold~1, and otherwise ResEnc-M was
retained. $R$ is
a rank statistic, a different object from $U_\tau$, a mean of raw metric values; the two are never
combined, and neither is the challenge's ranking procedure, which uses the Dice similarity
coefficient (DSC) and normalized surface distance (NSD) and excludes HD95, so screen and ranking
metric do not agree;
Section~\ref{sec:arch2} rescores the same predictions and Section~\ref{sec:disc} records the
mismatch as a limitation.

\subsection{Release Inference Path}
Final inference is a sequential five-checkpoint mean-probability ensemble: one fold model is resident
at a time and freed before the next, so simultaneous model residency does not scale with the five
folds. That bounds model residency only; the running probability accumulator and the current
per-region probabilities are also held, so process peak is not that of a bare single-model run.
Tiles are extracted with a sliding window at step $0.5$ of the patch size and combined with Gaussian
importance weighting. Region probabilities
are thresholded at a fixed $0.5$---no learned threshold or ensemble weight, no test-time
augmentation, no component filtering, no presence gate---and reconstructed by a hierarchy-safe
projection enforcing $\mathrm{WT}\supseteq\mathrm{TC}\supseteq\mathrm{ET}$ at the exact source
geometry. The runner is near-deterministic but not bit-exact, because deterministic-algorithm
enforcement runs in warn-only mode; the supplement quantifies the residual variation.

\subsection{Decision Procedure for Policy Audits}
\label{sec:procedure}
Audits were staged (Fig.~\ref{fig:workflow}): each compared one frozen candidate with the release
baseline under prespecified rules, and development failure stopped advancement.

\paragraph{Which tolerance is authoritative.} Organizer-confirmed final ranking uses DSC and NSD at
$\tau=1$, \textbf{the authoritative analysis}, against which we report every decision; Panoptica's
$\tau=0.5$ default remains a prespecified sensitivity analysis only.

\paragraph{Utility.} Let $S_\tau$ be the subjects for which all six DSC/NSD components are finite
under \emph{both} policies, and $\bar m_{c,\tau}(P;S_\tau)$ the mean of component $c$ over $S_\tau$.
The audit utility is the unweighted arithmetic mean of those six \emph{raw} component means,
\begin{equation}
U_\tau(P;S_\tau)=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{c}\bar m_{c,\tau}(P;S_\tau),\qquad
\Delta U_\tau = U_\tau(P_{\mathrm{cand}};S_\tau)-U_\tau(P_{\mathrm{base}};S_\tau),
\end{equation}
with $c$ over ET/TC/WT $\times$ DSC/NSD$_\tau$. Since both policies share $S_\tau$, $\Delta U_\tau$ is
the equally weighted mean of six component deltas; unlike $R$ (Section~\ref{sec:arch}) it contains no
ranking, tie-breaking or HD95.

\paragraph{Common support and fail-closed evaluation.} A subject contributes only if all six
components are finite under both policies, so a change in scorable cases cannot itself create a gain;
we report $|S_\tau|$ beside every $\Delta U_\tau$. Empty reference regions follow the evaluator's
convention and non-finite entries are excluded, not imputed. Common support is a comparability, not
an error-tolerance, device: a candidate whose expected evaluation is missing, errored or
membership-mismatched is ineligible outright and the audit fails closed. Three of the twenty-three
holdout checks enforce this, and all three passed.

\paragraph{Uncertainty and safety.} Uncertainty is a paired \emph{subject-level} bootstrap---subjects
resampled, never region-cases---with seed $20260730$, $10{,}000$ resamples, a percentile interval and
the fraction of resamples with $\Delta U_\tau>0$; that fraction is the share in which the candidate is
ahead, not a posterior probability. Ties resolve to the baseline. Safety uses 26-connected lesion
components in the spirit of instance-wise evaluation~\cite{kofler2023panoptica}, metric choice
materially affecting such conclusions~\cite{reinke2024metricsreloaded}: a reference component counts
as missed if no predicted component of the same region overlaps it. The frozen margins were $0.0025$ on the point change in miss rate, $0.0050$ on its one-sided 95\%
upper bound and per region, and a 5\% cap on the relative increase in predicted false-positive
components. These are \emph{operational} margins fixed in the protocol, which records no clinical
derivation; they are not externally validated clinical thresholds.

\subsection{Container Contract}
\label{sec:contract}
The submission is a \texttt{linux/amd64} zero-network container for the official Task-3 budget
(NVIDIA A10G, 24\,GB VRAM, 12\,h), with all dependencies and five fold checkpoints baked in.
\textbf{Each output file name is the complete validated basename of its input case folder}, with no
truncation to a fixed-width identifier and no hardcoded cohort prefix; an earlier revision assuming a
fixed-width suffix was corrected, and the preflight tests multiple prefixes and variable-length
basenames. Labels are restricted to $\{0,1,2,3\}$, geometry is preserved exactly, and modality
discovery fails closed before any output is written. We make no claim that the runner refuses to
overwrite. The supplement carries the remaining operational detail. The source is public under the Apache License 2.0 at
\url{https://github.com/Neethan-hub/gat26-brats-2026/tree/brats-goat-2026-camera-ready-21-r11}; it
contains no images, labels, checkpoints or predictions.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Descriptive Out-of-Fold Performance}
\label{sec:oof}
Under the DSC/HD95 screen ResEnc-M won four of six fold-0 components, the paired bootstrap did not
favour ResEnc-L, and ResEnc-L failed both declared tail gates, so the policy selected ResEnc-M; the
supplement gives all six components.

In a strictly out-of-fold set---each case predicted once by the fold model that did not train on
it---the union covers 1{,}351 cases, each exactly once, with no missing case, duplicate or fold
overlap. Scored in one
pass by the official evaluator with zero errors and all six denominators equal to 1{,}351, it gives
DSC $0.865$ (ET), $0.915$ (TC), $0.927$ (WT) and HD95 $13.39$, $6.75$, $6.34$\,mm. This is
\emph{descriptive post-selection out-of-fold performance, conditional on the chosen model family and
split}---neither an unbiased generalization estimate nor in-sample accuracy: fold~0 chose the
architecture family and then appears inside this summary, and the corpus was reused across audits. It
is not the released ensemble's score, since every training case trained four of the five models and
it came from a different inference path.

\subsection{Rescoring the Architecture Comparison Under the Ranking Metric}
\label{sec:arch2}
Because the architecture screen used DSC/HD95, we rescored the same fold-0 predictions under DSC/NSD
at $\tau=0.5$ as a diagnostic. \textbf{The ordering reverses at the point estimate}: ResEnc-L is
better on four of six official components, ResEnc-M on TC and WT DSC. On the same statistic
$\Delta R = R(\mathrm{L})-R(\mathrm{M}) = -0.333$, nominally favouring ResEnc-L, but its
paired-bootstrap 95\% interval $[-1.000,+0.667]$ includes zero, so no rank advantage is established.
Fold-0 ResEnc-L weights already existed---they are what the diagnostic scores---so no five-fold
expansion, architecture change or retraining campaign followed. The diagnostic used existing fold-0
end-of-training predictions at $\tau=0.5$, not release-path predictions; the supplement gives all six
components. The ordering therefore reversed without a robust advantage, and deployed ResEnc-M had
been selected under a metric pair the challenge does not rank on.

\subsection{Two Inference Paths}
\label{sec:twopaths}
Every internal figure above came from the official evaluator applied to nnU-Net's own end-of-training
validation predictions, whose recorded settings include mirroring axes $(0,1,2)$: eightfold
mirroring test-time augmentation. The released container disables mirroring entirely. We found this by trying
to reproduce an internal development baseline through the release path and failing. On the same 811
development subjects, with the same evaluator and denominators, the augmented lineage reproduces the
recorded internal values to a worst absolute error of $4.3\times10^{-7}$, whereas the release path is
lower by roughly $0.002$ on each DSC component and $0.002$--$0.007$ on each NSD component. The gap is
single-signed across all twelve component/tolerance combinations, reproduces fold by fold, and is
orders of magnitude larger than the run-to-run variation we measured; it is consistent with the
mirroring-path difference and not with nondeterminism. No internal number here therefore estimates
what the released container produces.

\subsection{Checkpoint and Weight-Averaging Audits}
\label{sec:audits}
\textbf{Audit~A} tested the best-validation checkpoint, mirroring over the recorded axes, both
combined, and ET component cleanup at three declared volume thresholds; no candidate improved
robustly. \textbf{Audit~B} tested two declared per-fold weight averages of the final and
best-validation checkpoints~\cite{wortsman2022modelsoups} and is the sharpest negative: all six
DSC/NSD components moved the wrong way for both soups, though not monotonically---the $0.50$ soup
beat the $0.25$ one. Both stopped on
the development subset; per-component values are in the supplement.

\subsection{Mirroring: A Development Gain That Did Not Transfer Consistently}
\label{sec:auditc}
Audit~C tested one candidate, M8, differing from baseline C0 only in mirroring over axes exactly
$(0,1,2)$, executed end-to-end by the release machinery on raw NIfTI inputs. One scoping point is
essential: each subject is predicted by \emph{its one excluded-fold checkpoint}, whereas the deployed
submission ensembles \emph{all five}, so the audit tests a closely related inference-policy effect and
does not directly measure mirroring on the deployed five-model ensemble.

On the development subset M8 improved all six DSC/NSD components at both tolerances and seventeen of
eighteen frozen checks passed. The one failure was a strict
veto on any increase in lesion false-negative \emph{count}, which rose by 13 ($+0.3\%$); under that
frozen gate Audit~C \emph{stopped} and the baseline was retained. Because it stopped, the
candidate-side phases of that protocol---runtime qualification of M8, and any official submission of
M8---were conditional on advancement and were never reached. They are unreached branches of a frozen
protocol, not omitted steps, and they are unrelated to the separately documented official-validation
submission of the retained baseline~C0. Inspecting the veto exposed a limitation rather than an error: the number of reference lesion
components is \emph{policy-invariant}, but an absolute count neither normalizes by that fixed number
of opportunities nor carries an interval. We therefore fixed, in a pushed commit, a separate
reference-lesion miss-rate noninferiority analysis together with a follow-up examination of the same
frozen M8 candidate on the untouched folds 3--4. That
follow-up is \emph{development-informed}: it was designed after seeing Audit~C's development outcome
and commit-frozen before those folds were opened, so it is not globally result-blind and we say so
plainly. It is a separate subsequent analysis, not continuation or advancement under Audit~C's failed
gate, and it does not annul the earlier audit, which correctly applied its own frozen rule. Its
candidate-side phases were likewise conditional and were not reached.

\paragraph{Holdout outcome.} These holdout values come from that separate follow-up, and any
comparison against the development values is a descriptive cross-analysis rather than evidence that
one globally result-blind protocol carried through. At $\tau=1$ the follow-up gain was $+0.00073$
against the development $+0.00253$, below the
required $+0.0010$; the positive-resample fraction was $0.734$ against $1.000$ and the 95\% interval
$[-0.0025,+0.0026]$ straddles zero. The $\tau=0.5$ sensitivity analysis behaves the same way:
$+0.00247$ against $+0.00417$, fraction $0.961$, interval $[-0.0003,+0.0042]$, also including zero.
Descriptively the holdout retained $28.7\%$ of the development gain at $\tau=1$ and $59.3\%$ at
$\tau=0.5$; neither is an effect size. The two holdout folds gave $+0.0023$ and
$-0.0009$---a sign change---zero-DSC region cases rose from 24 to 26, and the reference-lesion miss
rate regressed by $+0.0070$ against the $0.0025$ margin, with a one-sided 95\% upper bound of
$+0.0124$ against the $0.0050$ margin and all three regions over their own limits. Eight of 23 checks
failed, including the primary utility and safety gates. No denominator and no non-finite pattern
changed, so neither the residual gain nor the failure is a denominator artifact, and the released
no-augmentation policy was retained.

\paragraph{What failed, qualitatively.} We publish no patient-level examples. In aggregate,
mirroring consistently \emph{reduced} predicted false-positive lesion components (development
$1243\rightarrow1087$; holdout $864\rightarrow781$) while consistently \emph{increasing} missed
reference components ($4103\rightarrow4116$; $2726\rightarrow2764$). That is consistent with a
\emph{possible} precision--sensitivity trade-off, but it remains only a hypothesis: we did not
stratify by lesion size and did not test the mechanism. What is established is the direction of the counts, which the overlap means---all improved---do not
reveal.

\paragraph{Bounded inventory.} Our search was bounded: a connected-component proposal, a 40-epoch
augmentation and tumor-type-mixture screen whose bound cannot speak to convergence, and an untested
dense-overlap variant (D25) were considered and none adopted; larger ensembles, cascades, cohort
routing and alternative thresholds were not tested. The supplement lists each with its outcome.

\subsection{Runtime Evidence}
\label{sec:container}
Our full-cohort evidence is the run of the exact release policy that produced
Table~\ref{tab:official}: 451 of 451 cases, exit code $0$, zero inference errors, peak
framework-reserved VRAM $2.48$\,GiB, wall time 3\,h\,35\,m ($28.6$\,s per case) on a single NVIDIA
A40. Both figures describe that A40 run only; the $2.48$\,GiB is framework-reserved memory, not total
device memory, and is neither an A10G measurement nor an A10G headroom claim. An
earlier, superseded revision was exercised on one A10G using synthetic volumes. That test does not
qualify the corrected image: its fixtures used fixed-width folder names and so never exercised the
variable-length condition the correction addresses. We hold \emph{no} A10G measurement of the corrected image, no organizer
execution log for that image, and no hidden-test performance result or rank for it. Eightfold mirroring multiplies forward passes per tile by eight; we measured neither its
full-ensemble runtime nor memory profile on the target platform and make no feasibility claim in
either direction.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:disc}
Our quantities are not interchangeable: out-of-fold cross-validation is descriptive and
post-selection, audit results are policy-selection comparisons rather than performance estimates, the
single official measurement carries no rank, and hidden-test performance, which alone determines the
ranking, does not exist at the time of writing, so we report and estimate none. The procedure did
what it was built to do---a
candidate improving all six DSC/NSD components on the development subset, every $\tau=1$ resample
positive, was stopped before release by gates fixed in advance---but this is one demonstration on one
system. We have not shown that the gates generalize, that the margins suit another model or corpus,
or that the procedure outperforms nested cross-validation, which we did not run.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The policy holdout is same-corpus: folds 3--4 carry prior development
history, so they bound direct post-result tuning rather than adaptive reuse of the corpus, and
multiplicity across audits is uncontrolled; we hold no external independent cohort. The
architecture--metric mismatch and its diagnostic reversal are as reported in
Section~\ref{sec:arch2}. The mirroring audit is a single-model out-of-fold comparison
(Section~\ref{sec:auditc}) and does not determine how mirroring would affect the deployed
five-checkpoint ensemble, which could mitigate, preserve or amplify the lesion-miss behaviour; we
neither adopted it nor claim it would have helped. The absent per-case row, the
non-derivability of per-case cohort labels, and the non-attribution of the official gap are as
stated in Section~\ref{sec:official}; the absence of any hidden-test result or rank, organizer
execution log or A10G measurement for the corrected image is as stated in
Section~\ref{sec:container}. Group-level splitting cannot prove the absence of same-subject leakage, a small minority of cases
dominates the boundary-error mean, and the development-subset bootstrap exists only at $\tau=1$.
GAT-26 trains only on GoAT-provided data from a recorded random initialization, with no external,
prior-BraTS, private or pretrained data or weights; validation and test labels were never used, and
we performed no pseudo-labeling, transductive adaptation or leaderboard-driven tuning.

\section{Conclusion}
GAT-26 is a five-fold region-based residual-encoder nnU-Net ensemble whose release policy was
selected and released following commit-frozen rules. All three audits stopped on the development
subset; for the mirroring candidate a separate, development-informed follow-up then examined the
untouched same-corpus policy-selection holdout, where the gain was descriptively smaller at both
tolerances, both intervals spanned zero, one fold changed sign and a lesion miss-rate gate failed.
The simpler baseline was retained. Two observations travel further than the architecture: internal
cross-validation numbers and the shipped container came from different inference paths, and a
development-subset gain that looked unambiguous was only partially reproduced on the
holdout---$28.7\%$ at the official-ranking-aligned $\tau=1$---while lesion-miss behaviour worsened.
Official validation fell well below our internal figures, a gap we report without attributing. This is
a bounded case study.
