\begin{abstract}
% No citations permitted in the abstract.
We ask whether, for a fixed containerized segmentation system, a commit-frozen multi-gate policy
audit can prevent adoption of an inference modification whose aggregate development-subset benefit
does not transfer to a same-corpus policy-selection holdout. GAT-26 is a five-fold region-based
residual-encoder nnU-Net trained only on subchallenge data from a recorded random initialization. Our
contribution is procedural: the inference policy that ships is the object under test, and candidates,
utility and gates were fixed in a pushed commit before the specified comparison. The utility is the unweighted mean of six raw component means, ET/TC/WT by
DSC and NSD, on common subject support. Eightfold mirroring improved all six DSC/NSD components on the
development folds, then weakened on the holdout: at the official-ranking tolerance the gain fell from
$+0.00253$ to $+0.00073$, and in a sensitivity analysis at the lower tolerance from $+0.00417$ to
$+0.00247$. On the holdout the bootstrap interval spanned zero at both tolerances, one of the two
folds changed sign, and the reference-lesion miss rate regressed by $+0.0070$ against a $0.0025$
operational margin, so the simpler baseline was retained. We also document a discrepancy that is easy to ship
unnoticed: our internal cross-validation used eightfold mirroring while the released container does
not, so the two are not comparable. This is a bounded case study, not evidence that the
procedure is preferable to nested validation.

\section{Introduction}
The Brain Tumor Segmentation (BraTS) benchmark has standardized evaluation of automated glioma
segmentation from multiparametric MRI~\cite{menze2015brats,bakas2017advancing,baid2021brats} and has
expanded into subchallenges covering meningioma~\cite{bratsmen2023}, brain
metastases~\cite{bratsmet2023}, pediatric tumors~\cite{bratsped2023} and an under-represented
Sub-Saharan Africa patient population~\cite{bratsssa2023}, scored by containerized
infrastructure~\cite{karargyris2023medperf}. BraTS-GoAT poses the cross-tumor question directly: one
model must segment enhancing tumor (ET), tumor core (TC) and whole tumor (WT) across heterogeneous
tumor types and acquisition sources, with no per-cohort tuning and no cohort label at inference time.

Strong baselines are well established and the self-configuring nnU-Net family remains hard to beat on
BraTS-style tasks~\cite{isensee2021nnunet,isensee2024nnunetrevisited}, so we did not pursue a new
architecture. What interested us instead is that a challenge submission is not a model but a
\emph{container}, and that the inference policy inside it---checkpoint choice, test-time
augmentation, tiling overlap, thresholding, post-processing, ensembling---is routinely chosen on the
same data used to report cross-validated performance, with no holdout and no advance commitment.
Underspecification makes this fragile: pipelines equivalent on a development distribution can differ
elsewhere~\cite{damour2022underspecification}.

\paragraph{Research question.} We ask: \emph{for a fixed containerized segmentation system, can a
commit-frozen, multi-gate policy audit prevent adoption of an inference modification whose aggregate
development-subset benefit does not transfer consistently to a same-corpus policy-selection
holdout?} We answer it as a bounded case study on one system, corpus and split, and do not claim it
shows the procedure is generally superior to nested cross-validation or an external cohort.

\paragraph{Transferable conclusions.} Three claims survive our evidence. \emph{Release-path
equivalence must be verified before inference policies are compared}---ours was not, which silently
offsets every internal figure. \emph{A small aggregate gain can coexist with fold heterogeneity and
worse lesion-miss behaviour}, so an aggregate mean is not sufficient evidence to adopt a change. And
\emph{a same-corpus policy holdout reduces direct post-result tuning}, but it is
not an external independent cohort and does not replace nested validation.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data and Task}
We use only data supplied through the 2026 BraTS-GoAT subchallenge, which draws on the BraTS data
lineage~\cite{menze2015brats,bakas2017advancing,baid2021brats}, its expert-annotated public
collections~\cite{bakas2017gbm,bakas2017lgg} and its cohort-specific
releases~\cite{bratsmen2023,bratsmet2023,bratsped2023,bratsssa2023}. The training release comprises
1{,}351 labeled cases, which our audit found complete, geometry-valid and integer-labeled; evaluated
regions are the standard nested triple $\mathrm{ET}=[3]$, $\mathrm{TC}=[1,3]$, $\mathrm{WT}=[1,2,3]$.
Validation and test ground truth is withheld. Per-case cohort labels were not reliably derivable and
we did not guess them, so cohort-stratified analysis lies outside this work---a real limitation for a
task about cohort transfer.

\subsection{Split, Preprocessing and Training}
We build a deterministic group-level five-fold split (seed $21072026$; fold sizes 271, 270, 270, 270
and 270), frozen before training and never changed. A duplicate analysis found no confirmed
same-subject pairs, so cases are treated as singleton groups; we claim only that the audit found no
confirmed duplicate group, not that leakage is impossible. Every fold contains ET-absent and
TC-absent cases, exercising the empty-region paths the evaluator must handle.

Preprocessing is the standard nnU-Net v2.8.1 pipeline over the four modalities (T1n, T1c, T2w, T2f)
in a fixed channel order. The backbone is the ResEnc-M residual-encoder U-Net in
\texttt{3d\_fullres} with region-based ET/TC/WT targets, patch size $[128,160,112]$, batch size 2,
SGD with Nesterov momentum, initial learning rate $10^{-2}$ with polynomial decay, 0.33 foreground
oversampling and deep supervision, trained 1000 epochs per fold with the final checkpoint retained;
the full plan is in the public repository. All parameters begin from a recorded random
initialization; no pretrained or self-supervised weights are used. Folds train independently, and all
five are complete.

\subsection{Architecture Selection, and Its Metric}
\label{sec:arch}
Selection between the ResEnc-M and ResEnc-L presets followed a policy fixed before any fold result
was seen, on fold~0 only, using DSC and HD95. Its statistic differs from the audit utility. For each
of the six screened components the presets are ranked against each other, better value first (higher
DSC, lower HD95), exact ties taking the average of the tied positions; $R$ is the mean of those six
fractional ranks, so \emph{lower $R$ is better}. We report $\Delta R=R(\mathrm{L})-R(\mathrm{M})$,
negative favouring ResEnc-L, with a paired subject-level bootstrap (seed $21072026$, $10{,}000$
resamples) summarized by a 95\% percentile interval; ResEnc-L is preferred only if that interval
excludes zero and every declared noninferiority gate passes. $R$ is a rank statistic, a different
object from $U_\tau$, which is a mean of raw metric values; the two are never combined, and neither
is the challenge's ranking procedure. The challenge ranks the Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) and
the normalized surface distance (NSD) and excludes HD95, so the screen and the ranking metric do not
agree;
Section~\ref{sec:arch2} rescores the same predictions and Section~\ref{sec:disc} records the
mismatch as a limitation.

\subsection{Release Inference Path}
Final inference is a sequential five-checkpoint mean-probability ensemble: one fold model is resident
at a time and freed before the next, so peak memory is that of a single model. Region probabilities
are thresholded at a fixed $0.5$---no learned threshold or ensemble weight, no test-time
augmentation, no component filtering, no presence gate---and reconstructed by a hierarchy-safe
projection enforcing $\mathrm{WT}\supseteq\mathrm{TC}\supseteq\mathrm{ET}$ at the exact source
geometry. The runner is near-deterministic but not bit-exact, because deterministic-algorithm
enforcement runs in warn-only mode; the supplement quantifies the residual variation.

\subsection{Decision Procedure for Policy Audits}
\label{sec:procedure}
Candidate evaluation is \emph{staged} (Fig.~\ref{fig:workflow}). Each audit compares one frozen
candidate against the frozen release baseline under rules fixed in advance, using the organizers'
evaluation package; a candidate failing its advancement criteria stops on the development subset and
is not carried further.

\paragraph{Which tolerance is authoritative.} The organizers confirmed that the final ranking uses
DSC and NSD, excludes HD95, and computes final-ranking NSD at $\tau=1$. We therefore treat
\textbf{$\tau=1$ as the official-ranking-aligned analysis} and report every decision against it.
Panoptica's default is $\tau=0.5$, which our earlier runs used; we retain it throughout as a
prespecified sensitivity analysis, which does not carry equal official standing.

\paragraph{Utility.} Let $S_\tau$ be the subjects for which all six DSC/NSD components are finite
under \emph{both} policies, and $\bar m_{c,\tau}(P;S_\tau)$ the mean of component $c$ over $S_\tau$.
The audit utility is the unweighted arithmetic mean of those six \emph{raw} component means,
\begin{equation}
U_\tau(P;S_\tau)=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{c}\bar m_{c,\tau}(P;S_\tau),\qquad
\Delta U_\tau = U_\tau(P_{\mathrm{cand}};S_\tau)-U_\tau(P_{\mathrm{base}};S_\tau),
\end{equation}
with $c$ over ET/TC/WT $\times$ DSC/NSD$_\tau$. Since both policies share $S_\tau$, $\Delta U_\tau$ is
identically the arithmetic mean of the six component deltas. DSC and NSD enter with equal weight, no
region is up-weighted, and HD95 never enters $U_\tau$. \emph{$U_\tau$ is a metric mean, not a rank
statistic}: no ranking or tie-breaking is involved, which distinguishes it from the fold-0
architecture statistic $R$ of Section~\ref{sec:arch}.

\paragraph{Common support and fail-closed evaluation.} A subject contributes only if all six
components are finite under both policies, so a change in the number of scorable cases cannot by
itself create a gain; we report $|S_\tau|$ beside every $\Delta U_\tau$. Empty reference regions
follow the evaluator's convention and non-finite entries are excluded, not imputed. Common support is
a comparability device, not an error-tolerance device: a candidate whose expected evaluation is
missing, errored or membership-mismatched is ineligible outright, and the audit fails closed. Three
of the twenty-three holdout checks enforce exactly this, and all three passed.

\paragraph{Uncertainty and safety.} Uncertainty is a paired \emph{subject-level} bootstrap---subjects
are resampled, never region-cases---with seed $20260730$, $10{,}000$ resamples, a percentile interval
and the fraction of resamples with $\Delta U_\tau>0$; that fraction is the share of resamples in which
the candidate is ahead, not a posterior probability. Ties resolve to the baseline. Safety is assessed
on 26-connected lesion components in the spirit of instance-wise
evaluation~\cite{kofler2023panoptica}, and metric choice materially affects such
conclusions~\cite{reinke2024metricsreloaded}: a reference component counts as missed if no predicted
component of the same region overlaps it. The frozen margins were $0.0025$ on the point change in
miss rate, $0.0050$ on its one-sided 95\% upper bound and per region, and a 5\% cap on the relative
increase in predicted false-positive components. These are \emph{operational} margins fixed in the
protocol, which records no clinical derivation for them; they are not externally validated clinical
thresholds.

\subsection{Container Contract}
\label{sec:contract}
The submission is a \texttt{linux/amd64} zero-network container built for the official Task-3 budget
(NVIDIA A10G, 24\,GB VRAM, 12\,h), with all dependencies and all five fold checkpoints baked in at
build time. It reads a read-only \texttt{/input} and writes a flat \texttt{/output} holding one
\texttt{.nii.gz} per case folder. \textbf{Each output file name is the complete validated basename of
its input case folder}, with no truncation to a fixed-width identifier and no hardcoded cohort
prefix; an earlier revision that assumed a fixed-width suffix was corrected, and the release
preflight now tests multiple prefixes and variable-length basenames. Labels are restricted to
$\{0,1,2,3\}$, geometry is preserved exactly, and modality discovery fails closed before any output
is written. The official contract supplies a fresh writable \texttt{/output}; we make no claim that
the runner refuses to overwrite an existing file. The supplement carries the remaining operational
detail. The source is public under the Apache License 2.0 at
\url{https://github.com/Neethan-hub/gat26-brats-2026/tree/brats-goat-2026-camera-ready-21-r3}; it
contains no images, labels, checkpoints or predictions.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Descriptive Out-of-fold Performance}
\label{sec:oof}
Under the DSC/HD95 screen ResEnc-M won four of six fold-0 components, the paired bootstrap did not
favour ResEnc-L, and ResEnc-L failed both declared tail gates, so the policy selected ResEnc-M; the
supplement gives all six components.

We assembled a strictly out-of-fold set in which each case is predicted once, by the fold model that
did not train on it: the union covers 1{,}351 cases, each exactly once, with no missing case,
duplicate or fold overlap. Scored in one pass by the official evaluator with zero errors and all six
denominators equal to 1{,}351, it gives DSC $0.865$ (ET), $0.915$ (TC), $0.927$ (WT) and HD95
$13.39$, $6.75$, $6.34$\,mm. This is \emph{descriptive post-selection out-of-fold performance,
conditional on the chosen model family and split}---neither an unbiased generalization estimate nor
in-sample training accuracy: fold~0 chose the architecture family and then appears inside this
summary, and the corpus was reused across audits. It is not the released ensemble's score and cannot
be, since every training case trained four of the five constituent models, and it came from a
different inference path.

\subsection{Rescoring the Architecture Comparison under the Ranking Metric}
\label{sec:arch2}
Because the architecture screen used DSC/HD95, we rescored the same fold-0 predictions under DSC/NSD
at $\tau=0.5$ as a diagnostic. \textbf{The ordering reverses at the point estimate}: ResEnc-L is
better on four of six official components, ResEnc-M on TC and WT DSC. On the same statistic
$\Delta R = R(\mathrm{L})-R(\mathrm{M}) = -0.333$, nominally favouring ResEnc-L, but its
paired-bootstrap 95\% interval $[-1.000,+0.667]$ includes zero, so no rank advantage is established.
Fold-0 ResEnc-L weights already existed---they are what the diagnostic scores---so the point is not
that no ResEnc-L was ever trained, but that no five-fold expansion, architecture change or retraining
campaign followed it. The diagnostic is fold-0 only, at $\tau=0.5$ only, and scores end-of-training
rather than release-path predictions; the supplement gives all six components. We therefore do
\emph{not} claim the architecture conclusion is unchanged under the ranking metric: the ordering
reverses, the reversal is not robust, and the deployed model was selected under a metric pair the
challenge does not rank on.

\subsection{Two Inference Paths}
\label{sec:twopaths}
Every internal figure above came from the official evaluator applied to nnU-Net's own end-of-training
validation predictions, whose recorded settings include mirroring axes $(0,1,2)$: eightfold mirroring
test-time augmentation. The released container disables mirroring entirely. We found this by trying
to reproduce an internal development baseline through the release path and failing. On the same 811
development subjects, with the same evaluator and denominators, the augmented lineage reproduces the
recorded internal values to a worst absolute error of $4.3\times10^{-7}$, whereas the release path is
lower by roughly $0.002$ on each DSC component and $0.002$--$0.007$ on each NSD component. The gap is
single-signed across all twelve component/tolerance combinations, reproduces fold by fold, and is
orders of magnitude larger than the run-to-run variation we measured; it is consistent with the
mirroring-path difference and not with nondeterminism. No internal number here therefore estimates
what the released container produces.

\subsection{Checkpoint and Weight-averaging Audits}
\label{sec:audits}
\textbf{Audit~A} tested the best-validation checkpoint, mirroring over the recorded axes, both
combined, and ET component cleanup at three declared volume thresholds; no candidate improved
robustly. \textbf{Audit~B} tested two declared per-fold weight averages of the final and
best-validation checkpoints~\cite{wortsman2022modelsoups} and is the sharpest negative: all six
DSC/NSD components moved the wrong way for both soups, monotonically in the amount mixed in. Both stopped on
the development subset; per-component values are in the supplement.

\subsection{Mirroring: a Development Gain That Did Not Transfer}
\label{sec:auditc}
Audit~C tested a single candidate, M8, differing from the baseline C0 only in that mirroring is
enabled over axes exactly $(0,1,2)$, executed end-to-end by the release machinery on raw NIfTI
inputs. One scoping point is essential: here each subject is predicted by \emph{its one excluded-fold
checkpoint}, whereas the deployed submission ensembles \emph{all five}. The audit tests a closely
related inference-policy effect; it does not directly measure mirroring on the deployed five-model
ensemble.

On the development subset M8 improved all six DSC/NSD components at both tolerances and
seventeen of eighteen frozen checks passed. The one failure was a strict
veto on any increase in lesion false-negative \emph{count}, which rose by 13 ($+0.3\%$); under that
frozen gate the audit retained the baseline. Inspecting it exposed a limitation of the veto rather
than an error in it: the number of reference lesion components is \emph{policy-invariant}, but an
absolute count neither normalizes by that fixed number of opportunities nor carries an interval. We therefore
fixed, in a pushed commit, a separate reference-lesion miss-rate noninferiority analysis. It was
commit-frozen before the holdout comparison but is not globally result-blind---it was designed after
seeing the development outcome, and we say so plainly---and it does not annul the earlier audit,
which correctly applied its own frozen rule.

\paragraph{Holdout outcome.} At $\tau=1$ the gain fell from $+0.00253$ to $+0.00073$, below the
required $+0.0010$; the positive-resample fraction fell from $1.000$ to $0.734$ and the 95\% interval
$[-0.0025,+0.0026]$ straddles zero. The $\tau=0.5$ sensitivity analysis moves the same way:
$+0.00417$ to $+0.00247$, fraction $0.961$, interval $[-0.0003,+0.0042]$, also including zero.
Descriptively the holdout retained $28.7\%$ of the development gain at $\tau=1$ and $59.3\%$ at
$\tau=0.5$; neither ratio is an effect size. The two holdout folds gave $+0.0023$ and
$-0.0009$---a sign change---zero-DSC region cases rose from 24 to 26, and the reference-lesion miss
rate regressed by $+0.0070$ against the $0.0025$ margin, with a one-sided 95\% upper bound of
$+0.0124$ against the $0.0050$ margin and all three regions over their own limits. Eight of 23 checks
failed, including the primary utility and safety gates. No denominator and no non-finite pattern
changed, so neither the residual gain nor the failure is a denominator artifact, and the released
no-augmentation policy was retained.

\paragraph{What failed, qualitatively.} We publish no patient-level examples, so we characterize
failure in aggregate. Mirroring consistently \emph{reduced} predicted false-positive lesion components
(development $1243\rightarrow1087$; holdout $864\rightarrow781$) while consistently \emph{increasing}
missed reference components ($4103\rightarrow4116$; $2726\rightarrow2764$). That is consistent with a
precision--sensitivity trade-off, but we offer it only as a hypothesis: we did not stratify by lesion
size and did not test the mechanism. What is established is the direction of the counts, which the
overlap means---all of which improved---do not reveal.

\paragraph{Bounded inventory.} Our search was bounded. A connected-component proposal, a
40-epoch fine-tuning screen of three augmentation and tumor-type-mixture variants, and a
dense-overlap variant were considered and none adopted, and the 40-epoch bound means that screen
cannot speak to convergence behaviour. We did not test larger ensembles, cascades, cohort routing or
alternative thresholds. The supplement lists each with its outcome.

\subsection{Runtime Evidence}
\label{sec:container}
Our full-cohort evidence is the run of the exact release policy whose predictions produced
Table~\ref{tab:official}: 451 of 451 cases, exit code $0$, zero inference errors, peak
framework-reserved VRAM $2.48$\,GiB, wall time 3\,h\,35\,m ($28.6$\,s per case) on a single NVIDIA
A40. Both figures describe that A40 execution only; the $2.48$\,GiB is memory reserved by the tensor
framework, not total device memory, and is neither an A10G measurement nor an A10G headroom claim. An
earlier, superseded revision was exercised on one A10G using synthetic volumes; that test does not
qualify the corrected image, whose fixed-width fixtures never exercised the variable-length condition
the correction addresses. We hold \emph{no} A10G measurement of the corrected image, no organizer
execution log and no hidden-test evidence. Eightfold mirroring multiplies forward passes per tile by
eight, and we measured neither its full-ensemble runtime nor its memory profile on the target
platform, so we make no feasibility claim in either direction.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:disc}
Our quantities are not interchangeable: out-of-fold cross-validation is descriptive and
post-selection, from the augmented internal path; audit results are policy-selection comparisons, not
performance estimates; the single official measurement came from the release path on a withheld
cohort and carries no rank; and hidden-test performance, which alone determines the ranking, does not
exist at the time of writing, so we report and estimate none.

Returning to our question: the procedure did what it was built to do---a candidate that improved all
six DSC/NSD components on the development subset, with every $\tau=1$ bootstrap resample positive,
was stopped before release by gates fixed in advance. But this is one demonstration on one system. We
have not shown that the gates generalize, that the margins suit another model or corpus, or that the
procedure outperforms nested cross-validation, which remains preferable where feasible and which we
did not run.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The policy holdout is same-corpus: folds 3--4 carry prior development
history, so they bound direct post-result tuning of this comparison, not adaptive reuse of the
corpus, and multiplicity across our audits is uncontrolled. We hold no external independent cohort.
The architecture was selected under DSC/HD95 while the challenge ranks DSC/NSD, and the rescoring in
Section~\ref{sec:arch2} reverses the point ordering without robustness. The mirroring audit is a
single-model out-of-fold comparison and does not determine how mirroring would affect the deployed
five-checkpoint ensemble, which could mitigate, preserve or amplify the lesion-miss behaviour; we
neither adopted mirroring nor claim it would have helped. One submitted validation prediction is
absent from the organizers' per-case output. Cohort labels were not derivable, so no
cohort-stratified analysis was possible in a task about cohort transfer. We hold no hidden-test
evidence, no rank and no A10G measurement of the corrected image, and did not run the comparison that
would show whether the procedure outperforms nested validation. Group-level splitting cannot prove
the absence of same-subject leakage, a small minority of cases dominates the boundary-error mean, and
the development-subset bootstrap exists only at $\tau=1$. GAT-26 trains only on GoAT-provided data
from a recorded random initialization, with no external, prior-BraTS, private or pretrained data or
weights; validation and test labels were never used, and we performed no pseudo-labeling,
transductive adaptation or leaderboard-driven tuning.

\section{Conclusion}
GAT-26 is a five-fold region-based residual-encoder nnU-Net ensemble evaluated under commit-frozen
selection rules. Two audits stopped on the development subset; the mirroring audit advanced to a
same-corpus policy-selection holdout, where its gain weakened at both tolerances, its interval
spanned zero, one fold changed sign and a lesion miss-rate gate failed. All three retained the
simpler baseline. Two observations travel further than the architecture: internal cross-validation
numbers and the shipped container came from different inference paths, and a development-subset gain
that looked unambiguous transferred only partially---$28.7\%$ at the official-ranking tolerance
$\tau=1$---while lesion-miss behaviour worsened. Official validation fell well below our internal
figures, a gap we report without attributing. This is a bounded case study; nested or external
validation remains preferable where feasible.
