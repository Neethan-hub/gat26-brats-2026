\begin{abstract}
% No citations permitted in the abstract (wiki/639582).
We describe GAT-26, a submission to the BraTS 2026 Generalizability Across Tumors (BraTS-GoAT)
subchallenge, Task 3: pre-operative cross-tumor segmentation of enhancing tumor, tumor core and whole
tumor from multiparametric MRI. GAT-26, for Generalization-Aware Tri-region 2026, is built on the
official nnU-Net residual-encoder U-Net with region-based training, trained only on subchallenge data
from a recorded random initialization, with no external images or pretrained weights. Our contribution
is procedural rather than architectural: the inference policy that actually ships is the object under
test, and candidate definitions and decision rules were commit-frozen before candidate-specific
predictions and comparative metrics were computed. Evaluation was staged. Two audits, of checkpoint
choice and weight averaging, stopped at calibration and retained the baseline. A mirroring candidate
showed a calibration gain and so advanced to a same-corpus policy-selection holdout, where it was also
rejected: it had improved all six ranked components on the calibration folds with every paired
bootstrap resample positive, but retained about a quarter of that effect on the holdout, with an
interval spanning zero, a sign-flipping fold and a lesion miss-rate regression beyond its margin. We also document a discrepancy that is easy to ship unnoticed:
our internal cross-validation figures used eightfold mirroring augmentation while the released container
uses none, so the two are not comparable. Official validation Dice was 0.772, 0.824 and 0.879.
\keywords{Brain tumor segmentation \and Generalization \and nnU-Net \and Inference-policy
selection \and Reproducibility \and BraTS.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
For more than a decade the Brain Tumor Segmentation (BraTS) benchmark has standardized evaluation of
automated glioma segmentation from multiparametric
MRI~\cite{menze2015brats,bakas2017advancing,baid2021brats}, and has since expanded into
cohort-specific subchallenges covering meningioma~\cite{bratsmen2023}, brain
metastases~\cite{bratsmet2023}, pediatric tumors~\cite{bratsped2023} and an under-represented
Sub-Saharan Africa patient population~\cite{bratsssa2023}, scored by containerized infrastructure in
the spirit of federated benchmarking~\cite{karargyris2023medperf}. BraTS-GoAT poses the cross-tumor
question directly: one model must segment enhancing tumor (ET), tumor core (TC) and whole tumor (WT)
across these heterogeneous adult and pediatric tumor types and acquisition sources, with no per-cohort
tuning and no cohort label at inference time.

Strong baselines for this setting are well established, and the self-configuring nnU-Net family
remains hard to beat on BraTS-style tasks~\cite{isensee2021nnunet}, with residual-encoder presets
extending that result to larger compute budgets~\cite{isensee2024nnunetrevisited}. We therefore did
not pursue a new architecture. What interested us instead is that a challenge submission is not a
model but a \emph{container}, and that the inference policy inside it---checkpoint selection,
test-time augmentation, tiling overlap, thresholding, post-processing, ensembling---is routinely
chosen on the same data used to report cross-validated performance, with no holdout and no
pre-registration. Underspecification makes this fragile: pipelines equivalent on a development
distribution can differ elsewhere~\cite{damour2022underspecification}, exactly the regime a
generalization task probes.

Our contributions are: a conservative container-oriented cross-tumor system with an explicit release
contract; \emph{release-path verification}, checking whether the inference path that produced our
internal numbers is the path that ships, which here it was not; commit-frozen policy testing, in which
candidate, utility, gates and margins are fixed in a pushed commit before candidate-specific
predictions and comparative metrics are computed on the designated evaluation folds;
a concrete case in which a calibration gain with every bootstrap resample positive retained only about
a quarter of its size on a policy-selection holdout; and a transparent inventory of what we tried and
rejected. We claim no state-of-the-art accuracy, architectural novelty, clinical readiness, broad
generalization or competitive standing.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data and Task}
We use only data supplied through the 2026 BraTS-GoAT subchallenge, which draws on the BraTS data
lineage~\cite{menze2015brats,bakas2017advancing,baid2021brats}, its expert-annotated public
collections~\cite{bakas2017gbm,bakas2017lgg} and its cohort-specific
releases~\cite{bratsmen2023,bratsmet2023,bratsped2023,bratsssa2023}.
The training release comprises 1{,}351 labeled cases, which our acquisition audit found complete,
geometry-valid and integer-labeled. Labels follow the BraTS convention and the evaluated regions are
the standard nested triple:
\begin{equation*}
\{0{=}\text{bg},\,1{=}\text{NCR},\,2{=}\text{ED},\,3{=}\text{ET}\},\quad
\mathrm{ET}=[3],\quad \mathrm{TC}=[1,3],\quad \mathrm{WT}=[1,2,3].
\end{equation*}
Validation and test ground truth is withheld, so neither is used for training or tuning. Per-case
cohort labels were not reliably derivable from the released naming or metadata and we did not guess
them, so cohort-stratified analysis lies outside this work---a real limitation for a task about cohort
transfer.

\subsection{Duplicate-audited Split, Preprocessing and Training}
We build a deterministic group-level five-fold split (seed $21072026$). An automated duplicate and
near-duplicate analysis found no confirmed same-subject pairs, so cases are treated as singleton
groups; we claim only that the audit found no confirmed duplicate group and no tested anomaly, not
that same-subject leakage is impossible. Two independent runs produced byte-identical assignments,
fold sizes are $[271,270,270,270,270]$, and every fold contains ET-absent and TC-absent cases,
exercising the empty-region paths the evaluator must handle. The split was frozen before training and
never changed.

Preprocessing is the standard nnU-Net v2.8.1 pipeline over the four modalities (T1n, T1c, T2w, T2f)
in a fixed channel order, using nnU-Net's own fingerprint and plans, with no external statistics. The
backbone is the ResEnc-M residual-encoder U-Net in the \texttt{3d\_fullres} configuration with
region-based ET/TC/WT targets, patch size $[128,160,112]$, batch size 2, SGD with Nesterov momentum,
weight decay $3\times10^{-5}$, initial learning rate $10^{-2}$ with polynomial decay as fixed in our
committed training plan, 0.33 foreground oversampling and deep supervision, trained 1000 epochs per
fold with the final checkpoint retained. Quantities not recoverable from our committed record, such as
the per-fold validation loss curves, are omitted rather than inferred from defaults. All trainable
parameters begin from a recorded random initialization; no pretrained, foundation or self-supervised
weights are used. Folds train independently, with no cross-fold resume; all five are complete.

\subsection{Architecture Selection}
Selection between the ResEnc-M and ResEnc-L presets followed a policy fixed before any fold result was
seen: the utility is the mean fractional rank over six equally weighted components (ET/TC/WT $\times$
DSC/HD95), ties resolve to the smaller model, and ResEnc-L is preferred only on a meaningful fold-0
benefit---an award-rank gain $\ge 1/6$, a paired-bootstrap interval favoring L, and all pre-declared
noninferiority gates passing. The screen used fold 0 only, which matters for how the later
cross-validation figures should be read (Section~\ref{sec:oof}).

\subsection{Release Inference Path}
Final inference is a sequential five-checkpoint mean-probability ensemble: one fold model is resident
at a time and freed before the next, so peak memory is that of a single model. Region probabilities
are thresholded at a fixed $0.5$---no learned threshold, no learned ensemble weight, no test-time
augmentation, no connected-component filtering, no presence gate---and reconstructed by a
hierarchy-safe projection enforcing $\mathrm{WT}\supseteq\mathrm{TC}\supseteq\mathrm{ET}$, restoring
the exact source geometry with integer labels. Inference runs under enforced-determinism settings, and
we measured reproducibility rather than assuming it: across two independent 24-case runs, 18 of
$2.14\times10^{8}$ output voxels differed. The runner is therefore near-deterministic but not
bit-exact, because deterministic-algorithm enforcement runs in warn-only mode; every output remains
label-valid, hierarchy-consistent and geometry-exact.

\subsection{Decision Procedure for Policy Audits}
\label{sec:procedure}
Candidate evaluation is \emph{staged}. Every audit compares one frozen candidate policy against the
frozen release baseline under calibration rules fixed in advance, using the organizers' evaluation
package with explicit surface tolerances $\tau\in\{0.5,1.0\}$; a candidate failing its advancement
criteria stops at calibration and is not carried further. Audits~A and~B stopped there. The mirroring
candidate was first assessed under a frozen rule vetoing any increase in the lesion false-negative
count, failed it, and that audit likewise retained the baseline on calibration evidence alone. Only a
separate, explicitly calibration-informed protocol advanced that candidate to a policy-selection
holdout on folds 3--4, with its exact candidate, gates and margins fixed in a pushed commit before any
candidate-specific prediction or comparative metric was computed on them. The utility $U$ is the unweighted mean of the six
ranked components (ET/TC/WT $\times$ DSC/NSD) and $\Delta U$ is candidate minus baseline. Cases are
compared on \emph{common support}: a region-case contributes only if both policies yield a finite value,
so a change in the number of scorable cases cannot by itself create a gain, and we report the
common-support size beside every $\Delta U$; empty reference regions follow the evaluator's own
convention and non-finite entries are excluded rather than imputed. Uncertainty is a paired
\emph{subject-level} bootstrap---subjects are resampled, never region-cases---with seed $20260730$ and
$10{,}000$ resamples, summarized by the fraction of resamples with $\Delta U>0$ and a percentile
interval; that fraction is the share of resamples in which the candidate is ahead, not a posterior
probability. Ties resolve to the baseline. Safety is assessed on 26-connected lesion components: a
reference component counts as missed if no predicted component of the same region overlaps it, and the
miss rate is the fraction of reference components missed, a quantity whose denominator depends only on
the reference. Noninferiority margins were $0.0025$ on the point change, $0.0050$ on the one-sided 95\%
upper bound and per region, and a 5\% cap on the relative increase in predicted false-positive
components.

\subsection{Container Contract}
\label{sec:contract}
The submission is a \texttt{linux/amd64} zero-network container built for the official Task-3 budget
(NVIDIA A10G with 24\,GB VRAM, 16 vCPU, \texttt{--memory} 48\,GiB, \texttt{--shm-size} 16\,GiB, CUDA
$\le 13.0$, 12\,h total), with all dependencies and all five fold checkpoints baked at build time so
nothing is downloaded at runtime. It reads a read-only \texttt{/input} and writes a flat
\texttt{/output} holding one \texttt{.nii.gz} per case folder; output names are derived dynamically
from the input folder names and end in the five-digit case identifier, with no hardcoded cohort prefix.
Labels are restricted to $\{0,1,2,3\}$ and geometry is preserved exactly. Strict modality discovery
rejects missing, duplicate, unknown and unreadable modalities and invalid folder names before any
output is written, and the runner refuses to start without five distinct fold checkpoints. The official
contract supplies a fresh writable \texttt{/output} and our testing uses fresh output directories; we
make no claim that the runner refuses to overwrite a pre-existing output file. The source is public
under the Apache License 2.0 at the immutable tag
\url{https://github.com/Neethan-hub/gat26-brats-2026/tree/brats-2026-task3-final-source-r1}. It contains
no images, labels, checkpoints or predictions, and the trained weights are not redistributed, so the
repository does not reproduce training on its own.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Architecture Selection and Descriptive Out-of-fold Performance}
\label{sec:oof}
On the frozen fold-0 validation set (271 cases) ResEnc-M scored ET/TC/WT DSC of $0.859$, $0.914$ and
$0.934$ against ResEnc-L at $0.861$, $0.912$ and $0.932$, winning four of six components; the paired
bootstrap did not favor ResEnc-L (rank-difference interval $[-0.667,+0.667]$) and ResEnc-L failed both
pre-declared tail gates, so the policy selected ResEnc-M.

We assembled a strictly out-of-fold set in which each case is predicted once, by the fold model that
did not train on it: the union covers 1{,}351 cases, each exactly once, with no missing case, no
duplicate and no fold overlap, and each fold's realized predictions equal its frozen validation
membership. Scored in one pass by the official evaluator with zero errors and all six denominators
equal to 1{,}351, it gives DSC $0.865$ (ET), $0.915$ (TC), $0.927$ (WT) and HD95 $13.39$, $6.75$ and
$6.34$\,mm; the 5th-percentile DSC is $0.666$ and the 95th-percentile HD95 $17.72$\,mm.

Two qualifications are essential. First, this is \emph{descriptive post-selection} out-of-fold
performance, conditional on the chosen model family and split, not an unbiased estimate: fold 0 chose
the architecture family and then appears inside this summary, and the training corpus was reused
across audits, so adaptive selection and multiplicity can both create optimism. Second, it is not the
released ensemble's score and cannot be, since every training case trained four of the five
constituent models; it also came from a different inference path than the container ships.

\subsection{Two Inference Paths}
\label{sec:twopaths}
Every internal figure above came from the official evaluator applied to nnU-Net's own end-of-training
validation predictions. Those predictions record the settings that produced them, and the recorded
settings include mirroring axes $(0,1,2)$: eightfold mirroring test-time augmentation. The released
container builds its predictor with mirroring disabled and uses no test-time augmentation at all.

We found this by trying to reproduce an internal calibration baseline through the release code path
and failing. On the same 811 calibration subjects, with the same preserved evaluator, tolerances and
denominators, the augmented lineage reproduces the recorded internal values to a worst absolute error
of $4.3\times10^{-7}$, whereas the release path is lower by roughly $0.002$ on each Dice component and
$0.002$--$0.007$ on each NSD component. The gap is single-signed across all twelve
component/tolerance combinations and reproduces fold by fold --- the signature of test-time
augmentation, not of nondeterminism or evaluator variation. No internal number here therefore
estimates what the released container produces; relative conclusions among candidates sharing the same
augmented predictions are unaffected, but their absolute level is not the shipped level.

\subsection{Checkpoint and Weight-averaging Audits}
\label{sec:audits}
\textbf{Audit~A} tested the best-validation checkpoint, mirroring augmentation over the axes recorded
in the checkpoint metadata, both combined, and ET component cleanup at three pre-declared
physical-volume thresholds, on calibration folds 0--2 ($n=811$). No candidate improved robustly: the
augmentation candidate was point-positive but its bootstrap interval did not exclude a tie, and
component cleanup traded ET Dice against ET boundary distance for an exact six-component tie.
\textbf{Audit~B} tested two pre-declared per-fold weight averages of the final and best-validation
checkpoints, $S_1=0.75\theta_f+0.25\theta_b$ and $S_2=0.50\theta_f+0.50\theta_b$, in the spirit of
weight averaging along a training trajectory~\cite{wortsman2022modelsoups} and under strict structural
guards. It is the sharpest negative: every one of the six award components moved the wrong way for
both soups, monotonically in the amount of the best-validation checkpoint mixed in, the paired
bootstrap favored the baseline, and $S_1$ also failed the pre-declared 95th-percentile
boundary-distance gate. Both audits retained the baseline and left folds 3--4 untouched by any
candidate comparison.

\subsection{Mirroring Augmentation: a Calibration Gain That Did Not Hold Up}
\label{sec:auditc}
Audit~C tested a single candidate, M8, differing from the baseline C0 only in that mirroring is
enabled over axes exactly $(0,1,2)$. Both policies were executed by the release preprocessing,
prediction and reconstruction machinery on raw NIfTI inputs, so this is a release-path comparison
rather than a rerun of the internal validation lineage. One scoping point is essential: in this
out-of-fold audit each subject is predicted by \emph{its one excluded-fold checkpoint}, whereas the
deployed submission ensembles \emph{all five} checkpoints in their original order, so C0 and M8 differ
only in mirroring \emph{within that single-model comparison}. The audit tests a closely related
inference-policy effect; it does not directly measure mirroring on the final five-model ensemble.

On calibration, M8 improved all six ranked components at both tolerances (Table~\ref{tab:auditc}), and
seventeen of eighteen pre-declared checks passed. The one failure was a strict veto on any increase in
lesion false-negative \emph{count}, which rose by 13 ($+0.3\%$); that gate had been frozen before
calibration, and under it the audit correctly retained the baseline.

Inspecting that outcome exposed a limitation of the veto rather than an error in it. The number of
reference lesion components is \emph{policy-invariant} --- a property of the reference segmentation,
verified identical under both policies --- but an absolute missed-lesion count does not normalize the
change by that fixed number of opportunities and carries no uncertainty interval, so it cannot express
how large a regression is or how confidently it is established. We therefore preregistered a separate,
explicitly \emph{calibration-informed} reference-lesion miss-rate noninferiority analysis
(Section~\ref{sec:procedure}). It is confirmation-frozen but not globally result-blind: it was designed
after seeing the calibration outcome, and we say so plainly. It does not repair or annul the earlier
audit, which correctly applied its own frozen rule and stands as recorded.

The holdout was then evaluated. The $\tau=1$ gain fell from $+0.002532$ to $+0.000726$, below the
pre-declared $+0.0010$ requirement; the positive-resample fraction fell from $1.0000$ to $0.7342$ with
a 95\% interval straddling zero; the two holdout folds gave $+0.002323$ and $-0.000883$; zero-DSC region
cases rose from 24 to 26; and the miss rate regressed by $+0.006979$ against a $0.0025$ margin, with a
one-sided 95\% upper bound of $+0.012408$ against a $0.0050$ margin and every region over its own limit.
Eight of 23 holdout checks failed, including both the primary utility gate and the safety gate. No
denominator and no non-finite pattern changed between the policies, so neither the residual gain nor the
failure is a denominator artifact, and the released no-augmentation policy was retained. The pooled
all-fold estimate sits between the two; it reuses the folds that produced the decision, so we treat it
as supportive and post-selection.

\subsection{Bounded Experiment Inventory}
Our search was bounded, and we list it rather than implying exhaustiveness. A connected-component
proposal was rejected when its apparent mean gain proved to come from changed ET support. A 40-epoch
fine-tuning screen of three variants---a tumor-type-mixture schedule, a domain-generalization
augmentation schedule and their combination---failed its frozen gates. A dense-overlap variant (tile
step $0.5\rightarrow0.25$) was \emph{not} tested, because its baseline gate mixed an
augmentation-derived historical lineage with the no-augmentation release baseline. We did not test
larger ensembles, cascades, cohort routing or alternative thresholds, and make no claim about them.

\subsection{Runtime Evidence}
\label{sec:container}
Our full-cohort evidence is a run of the exact release inference policy, the run whose predictions
produced Table~\ref{tab:official}. It processed 451 of 451 official validation cases with exit code
$0$ and zero inference errors, at a peak reserved VRAM of $2.48$\,GiB, a total wall time of
3\,h\,35\,m and a mean of $28.6$\,s per case, on a single NVIDIA A40. All 17 output and archive gates
passed twice: over the prediction directory, and again over a fresh independent re-extraction. A
runtime measured on one GPU does not transfer to another, but this does establish that memory is far
from binding---a $2.48$\,GiB peak against a 24\,GB budget---because the ensemble runs sequentially.
We hold no measurement of the container on an NVIDIA A10G. The identity of the exact submitted image
is recorded with the challenge submission itself rather than printed here.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{A Hierarchy of Evidence, and What the Audit Does Not Establish}
Challenge papers often blur distinct quantities, so we separate ours. Out-of-fold cross-validation
(Section~\ref{sec:oof}) is descriptive and post-selection, produced by the augmented internal path, and
is not the released ensemble's score. Audit results are policy-selection comparisons on development
folds, not performance estimates. The single official measurement came from the release path on a
withheld cross-tumor cohort; it carries no rank and we do not convert it into an expected standing.
Hidden-test performance alone determines the ranking, is computed by the organizers after the deadline,
and does not exist at the time of writing; we report and estimate none. Cross-validation must not be
read as an official or hidden-test result, and internal and official figures are not comparable,
differing in both cohort and inference path.

The mirroring result is likewise a methodological observation, not a method: M8 is not part of the
release and is not offered as an improvement. Three limits bound it. It is a single-model out-of-fold
comparison, so it does not measure mirroring on the deployed five-checkpoint ensemble, and we have no
direct evidence the effect would behave identically there. Folds 3--4 come from the same corpus and
carry prior model-development history; they are a policy-selection holdout, not an external independent
cohort. And what the freeze buys is narrower than independence: the candidate-specific predictions and
comparative metrics were first computed after the protocol was pushed, which removes direct post-result
tuning of this comparison but not adaptive reuse of the corpus or multiplicity across our audits.

\subsection{Metric Considerations and Limitations}
The organizers rank Task 3 on the Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) and the normalized surface
distance (NSD); HD95 is diagnostic and excluded from the ranking. Our
original internal utility paired DSC with HD95, fixed before training. Once the ranking metric was
clarified we re-ran the entire pre-existing candidate set under a DSC/NSD utility using the
organizers' package, with the comparison protocol registered before any NSD value was computed, and
evaluated at both $\tau=0.5$ and $\tau=1.0$ rather than assuming one; the conclusion is unchanged at
either tolerance. More generally, metric choice and reporting materially affect such
conclusions~\cite{reinke2024metricsreloaded}, and our lesion-level analysis follows a
connected-component framework in the spirit of panoptic quality for medical
images~\cite{kofler2023panoptica}.

Group-level splitting mitigates but cannot prove the absence of same-subject leakage.
Cohort-stratified generalization, arguably the core question of this task, could not be analyzed
because per-case cohort labels were not derivable. Our internal figures came from a different inference
configuration than the released container. The policy holdout is same-corpus and we have no external
independent test cohort; one submitted validation prediction is absent from the organizers' per-case
output for undisclosed reasons; a small minority of cases dominates the boundary-error mean; and we
hold no hidden-test evidence. On data governance, GAT-26 trains only on GoAT-provided data
from a recorded random initialization, with no external, prior-BraTS, private or pretrained data or
weights, as the subchallenge rules require; validation and test labels were never used, and we
performed no pseudo-labeling, transductive adaptation or leaderboard-driven tuning.

\section{Conclusion}
GAT-26 is a five-fold region-based residual-encoder nnU-Net ensemble evaluated under commit-frozen
selection rules. The checkpoint and weight-averaging audits stopped at calibration; the mirroring audit
advanced to a same-corpus policy-selection holdout, where its apparent gain weakened and failed the
frozen utility and safety gates. All three retained the simpler baseline. Two findings travel better
than the architecture: a calibration improvement that looked unambiguous---every ranked component
better, every bootstrap resample positive---retained about a quarter of its size on the holdout, with
an interval spanning zero and a safety regression beyond its margin; and our internal cross-validation
numbers came from a different inference path than the container we ship, a discrepancy invisible unless
the release path is audited directly. Official validation performance was well below our internal
figures, which we report as a finding about cross-tumor transfer.
